<h2>Understanding Patient Access Restrictions</h2>
<p>Patients can restrict who accesses their NEHR information. When a patient
imposes a full access restriction, most of their information will be hidden
from view. However, certain essential clinical information, such as patient
demographics, allergies, and immunisation records, will remain visible
regardless of the restriction, as these are necessary for safe patient
care. Patients can also impose partial access restrictions that allow some
information to remain accessible while restricting other data based on
the type of information, specific healthcare providers, particular purposes,
or defined time periods.</p>
<h2>When You Can Override Restrictions</h2>
<p>You may access restricted information only when the access is necessary
to respond to an emergency that threatens the life or health of the patient,
whether or not the patient consents or is able to consent to the access
at that time. Certain information types prescribed by MOH may also be exempt
from restrictions and remain accessible regardless of patient-imposed blocks.</p>
<h2>Prohibited Actions</h2>
<p>You cannot access restricted information simply because a patient requests
it—patient consent does not override formally imposed restrictions. Similarly,
you cannot access restricted information for non-emergency purposes, even
if clinically useful. Restrictions must be respected unless you meet the
specific emergency override criteria.</p>
