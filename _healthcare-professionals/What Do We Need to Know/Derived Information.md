<h2>Disclosure of NEHR Information</h2>
<p>Only healthcare professionals involved in patient care may access NEHR,
and NEHR information may only be disclosed to other healthcare professionals
involved in caring for the same patient. As NEHR does not contain patients'
complete health records, healthcare professionals should direct patients
requesting NEHR information to view their health records through HealthHub
or contact the relevant healthcare provider that had provided the care
for which the information is being sought. Information from NEHR that has
been incorporated into an institution's medical records must be handled
in accordance with the Healthcare Services Act (HCSA), Personal Data Protection
Act (PDPA), and other applicable laws.</p>
<h2>Derived Information (For Research and Public Health)</h2>
<p>Derived information is aggregated or anonymised data extracted from NEHR
for research, public health, or policy purposes, distinct from regular
NEHR access for patient care. It falls into two categories:</p>
<ul data-tight="true" class="tight">
<li>
<p>Type 1: &nbsp;information that can identify individuals</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Type 2: &nbsp;aggregated or anonymised information that cannot identify
individuals</p>
</li>
</ul>
<h2>Who Can Access Derived Information</h2>
<p>Only MOH-approved organisations can access derived information through
a separate application process. This access is granted specifically for
research, public health surveillance, quality improvement initiatives,
or policy development that serves the broader public interest.</p>
<h2>When You Might Need Derived Information</h2>
<p>You may need derived information when conducting research studies requiring
population-level data, undertaking quality improvement projects needing
patient cohort data across multiple providers, participating in public
health investigations, or engaging in health services planning. These uses
extend beyond individual patient care and require analysis across multiple
patients or healthcare encounters.</p>
<h2>Application Process and Restrictions</h2>
<p>Submit an application to MOH specifying your intended purpose. MOH evaluates
requests based on public interest and necessity. If approved, you must
use the information only for approved purposes, cannot disclose it to others
without MOH approval, must delete it after your purpose is fulfilled, and
cannot attempt to re-identify individuals from anonymised data.</p>
<p>Most clinical care activities don't require derived information—they're
covered by your regular NEHR access rights. Derived information is specifically
for research, surveillance, and policy purposes beyond direct patient care</p>
