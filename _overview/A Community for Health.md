<h3>Slide 35: Annex C: Given wealth of information in NEHR, Minister may approve use of NEHR information for public health or public interest purposes, with safeguards</h3>
<p>• Minister may approve sharing of identifiable NEHR information for public
health purposes and sharing of non-identifiable NEHR information for public
interest purposes</p>
<p>• When sharing identifiable information, Minister may impose conditions
and restrictions to limit access to relevant NEHR information only e.g.
granting special accounts that limit NEHR access to certain records/data
types only</p>
<p>• Persons who gain access to NEHR information for these purposes must
comply with certain obligations e.g. ensuring information obtained is only
used or disclosed for purpose approved by Minister</p>
<p>• Misuse of NEHR information for such purposes will result in a penalty
of up to $50,000 and/or 2years’ imprisonment → penalty doubled if (i) it
is a repeat offence or (ii) an unauthorised person gained access to the
NEHR information</p>
<h3>Slide 36: Annex C: HIB provides legal basis for “A Community for Health” by enabling the sharing of identifiable health information outside NEHR with controls in place</h3>
<p>Part 3 of HIB can be used as basis for sharing relevant information outside
of NEHR if three conditions are met. Sharing limited to:</p>
<p>1) Between specified entities; To start with key public healthcare stakeholders,
i.e. AIC, healthcare clusters and public agencies. In future, could extend
to other private and community healthcare partners.</p>
<p></p>
<p>2) For specified use cases; and Use cases scoped to situations that support
continuity of care and outreach to benefit target populations</p>
<p></p>
<p>3) Confined to specified datatypes / sets for the use case.</p>
<p>-HIB uses “relevant information” as use cases may entail sharing health
information plus other identifiable data of individual or his/her caregiver</p>
<p>-This will be defined through subsidiary legislation, with prescribed
data types scoped to basic identification data and health risk indicators</p>
